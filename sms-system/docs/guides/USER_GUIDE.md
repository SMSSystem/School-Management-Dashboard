## Part 1 — Getting Started (All Users)

### 1.1 Logging In

1. Open the application in your web browser.
2. On the login screen, select your **Institution** from the dropdown first — this is required, and the Email/Password fields do not appear until you do. If you are a platform administrator rather than a member of a specific institution, choose **Platform Administration** instead.
3. Enter your **Email** and **Password**.
4. Click **Sign In**.
5. You will be redirected to your home dashboard automatically. The dashboard content you see depends on your role.

If the account you sign in with does not belong to the institution you selected, you will be signed back out and shown an error asking you to select the correct institution and try again.

An account can be signed in in **one place at a time**. If you try to sign in while the same account is already signed in on another device or browser tab, you are signed back out and told so. Sign out of the other place first (closing its tab also does this), or ask your Institution Administrator to clear the session (see Section 2.3).

If you cannot log in, contact your Institution Administrator to confirm your account details or reset your password.

---

### 1.2 The Dashboard Layout

Once logged in, the screen is divided into two areas:

- **Sidebar (left):** The main navigation menu. Click any item to go to that section. Items shown depend on your role — you will only see sections you have access to. On a phone the sidebar is a menu you open from the menu button at the top left of the header. Click a group heading (for example **PEOPLE**) to collapse or expand it; groups with only one page for you have nothing to collapse. Your choices are kept until you close the tab or sign out.
- **Main area (right):** The current page content.
- **Top bar (Navbar):** Displays your name on the right side. Also contains the theme toggle and the sign-out button.
- **Version:** The small number at the bottom of the sidebar (for example **v2.39.0**) is the version of the app you are using; it is also on the sign-in screen and under **Settings → About**. Include it when you report a problem.

Navigation is grouped into sections (e.g., **PEOPLE**, **CURRICULUM**, **TIMETABLE**, **OUTCOMES**, **REPORTS**, **ATTENDANCE**, **ADMINISTRATION**, **OTHER**).

---

### 1.3 Your User Profile

Your profile page lets you view and update your personal contact information.

1. In the sidebar, click **User Profile** (under the **OTHER** section).
2. Your name, email, phone number, and role are displayed.
3. To update your **Name** or **Phone**, click the **Edit** button, make your changes, and click **Save**.
4. Your **Email** cannot be changed here — contact your administrator if your email needs updating.

On a desktop or wide tablet (1024 px and up) the cards sit in two columns that end together, and the **Activity** card (below) spans the full width of the page; on a phone or narrow tablet the cards stack in one column.

#### Activity on your profile

The **Activity** card lists what has happened on your account, newest first, ten at a time. Use **Previous** and **Next** to move through older entries.

- **My activity** (everyone): your sign-ins and profile updates.
- **Actions I performed** (administrators and teachers): what _you_ changed on other people's records, for example "Updated phone for Jane Doe" or "Saved general attendance for Class 3A". It loads only when you open the tab. See Section 2.23 for what is recorded.

An entry marked **(unverified)** was saved before server-verified times existed, so its time was set by the browser and cannot be fully trusted.

---

### 1.4 Light and Dark Mode

The application supports both light mode and dark mode.

1. Click the **sun / moon icon** in the top-right corner of the Navbar to toggle between modes.
2. Your preference is saved automatically and remembered the next time you open the application.

---

### 1.5 Logging Out

1. Click the **sign-out icon** in the top-right corner of the Navbar.
2. You will be returned to the login screen immediately.

**Closing the browser tab also signs you out,** straight away. Reloading the page keeps you signed in, but a new tab does not share your sign-in: open pages in the same tab, or sign in again in the new one. If you are on a shared computer, close the tab (or click the sign-out icon) when you are done.

---

### 1.6 Inactivity Auto-Logout

If you are inactive, the application signs you out automatically: after **5 minutes** for teachers and administrators, and after **30 minutes** for students and parents. A warning appears 10 seconds before. Any unsaved changes will be lost, and you will need to sign in again to continue. Signing out also frees your account for signing in somewhere else.

---

## Part 2 — Institution Admin

As an Institution Admin, you have full access to all data within your school. This section covers every feature available to your role.

---

### 2.1 Setting Up Your Institution Profile

The Institution Profile stores your school's details and is required before you can generate report cards. A small amber dot appears next to the **Institution Profile** sidebar link until setup is complete.

1. In the sidebar, click **Institution Profile** (under **OTHER**).
2. The setup wizard has **7 steps**. Complete each step and click **Next** to proceed. You can also click **Back** to return to a previous step.

| Step               | What to fill in                                                                                        |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 1 — Basic Info     | Institution name (required) and optional motto                                                         |
| 2 — Contact        | Phone number, email address, and physical address                                                      |
| 3 — Logo           | Upload your institution logo (PNG or JPEG). The image is resized automatically.                        |
| 4 — Signature      | Upload an image of the authorised signatory's signature, or type a name as a text signature            |
| 5 — Role Labels    | Customise the staff role titles that appear on report cards (e.g., change "Principal" to "Headmaster") |
| 6 — Grading System | Choose **Flat** (all subjects equally weighted) or **Weighted** (subjects carry different weights)     |
| 7 — Review         | Confirm all entered details before saving                                                              |

3. On the **Review** step, click **Save** to finalise the profile.
4. The amber dot disappears once the profile is marked complete.

You can return to this page at any time to update your details. Changes take effect on the next report card generated.

---

### 2.2 Creating User Accounts

New accounts for teachers, students, and parents must be created by an Institution Admin.

1. In the sidebar, click **Create User** (under **PEOPLE**).
2. Select the **Role** for the new user: **Teacher** (Regular or Senior), **Student**, or **Parent**.
3. Fill in the required fields:
   - All roles: **Full Name**, **Email**, **Password**
   - Teachers: additional fields for staff information, including **Department** (required whenever your institution has at least one department — see Section 2.7)
   - Students: additional fields for student information
   - Parents: link to their child's student account
4. Click **Create** to create the account. The user can now sign in using the email and password you set.

> **Note:** Email addresses must be unique across the platform. Passwords should be communicated securely to the new user, who should change their password on first sign-in.

---

### 2.3 Managing Teachers

The Teachers list shows all teacher accounts registered to your institution.

1. In the sidebar, click **Teachers** (under **PEOPLE**).
2. Use the **search bar** at the top to filter by name or other fields.
3. Use the **Subject** menu to show only the teachers on one subject (or **No subjects** for teachers who are on none). Your choice is remembered until you sign out. A line above the table shows how many teachers match, for example _Showing 1-20 of 42 teachers (240 total)_; it updates as you change the menu or the search.
4. The list is paginated — click the page numbers at the bottom to navigate.

**To create a teacher**, use **Create User** (see Section 2.2) rather than the Teachers list directly.

**To edit a teacher's details:**

1. Click the **pencil (edit) icon** on the teacher's row.
2. Update the fields in the modal and click **Update**.

> **"No department" badge:** a teacher without a department shows an amber **No department** badge under their name. Senior Teachers can only see and edit notes and assignments written by Regular Teachers in _their own department_, so a teacher with no department is invisible to them. Open the teacher's edit form and choose a **Department** — it is required whenever your institution has departments, so you cannot save any other change to that teacher until you do. Assignments and exams the teacher saved earlier pick up the department the next time they are saved again.

**To delete a teacher:**

1. Click the **trash (delete) icon** on the teacher's row.
2. Confirm the deletion in the modal.

> Deleting a teacher account is permanent. Ensure the teacher has no outstanding records before deleting.

**To deactivate or reactivate a teacher** (the same controls appear on the Students and Parents lists):

1. Click the **person-with-an-X icon** on the row, or the **person-with-a-tick icon** to reactivate an inactive account.
2. Confirm in the dialog.

A deactivated account shows a red **Inactive** badge. Its owner is signed out and cannot sign in again until you reactivate it — when they try, the login page says _"This account is inactive. Contact your administrator to have it reactivated."_ Their records are kept. Unlike deleting, deactivation is fully reversible. The control is not offered on your own account, and each change is recorded in the Audit Log. A person who is deactivated while signed in is signed out the next time the app loads.

**Clearing a stuck sign-in session.** An account can only be signed in in one place at a time, and it is freed automatically when that tab closes. If a teacher, student or parent is told their account is _already signed in_ but they are not (for example after a crash), click the **sign-out icon** on their row (_Clear session_) and confirm. Their next sign-in works, and if they really are signed in somewhere else, that session is signed out. The change is recorded in the Audit Log. You cannot clear your own session, and only a Platform Administrator can clear another administrator's.

---

### 2.4 Managing Students

The Students list works identically to the Teachers list, with a **Class** menu (including **No class**) in place of the Subject menu.

On the other lists, **Subjects** has a **Teacher** menu (including **No teacher**), **Houses** has a **Class** menu (houses with a student in that class), and **Lessons** has a **Subject** menu next to its search box. The first column of **Subjects**, **Classes** and **Houses** is in natural order (1A, 2B, 10A; numbers before letters).

1. In the sidebar, click **Students** (under **PEOPLE**).
2. Search, edit, or delete student records using the same controls described in Section 2.3.

Deleting a student also removes their attempts, scores and extra-time entries in the online exams of the classes they are in now; the confirmation lists them ("exam attempts", "exam scores", "exam extra time"). The exams themselves stay.

To create a new student account, use **Create User** (see Section 2.2).

---

### 2.5 Managing Parents

1. In the sidebar, click **Parents** (under **PEOPLE**).
2. Parent records are created via **Create User** (see Section 2.2), where you link the parent to their child's student account.
3. Edit and delete controls follow the same pattern as Sections 2.3 and 2.4.

---

### 2.6 Managing Subjects

Subjects are the academic disciplines taught at your institution (e.g., Mathematics, English, Biology). Each subject has a **Coursework weight** and an **Exam weight**, which determine how final grades are calculated.

1. In the sidebar, click **Subjects** (under **CURRICULUM**).
2. Click the **+** button in the top-right corner to add a new subject.
3. Fill in the **Subject Name**, **Coursework weight (%)**, and **Exam weight (%)**.
   - The two weights must sum to 100.
4. Click **Create**.

To edit or delete a subject, use the pencil or trash icon on the subject's row.

> Subjects must be set up before you can record results or generate report cards for them.

Click the round **eye** button on a subject's row to see its description, attendance schedule, coursework and exam weights, its teachers and the classes it covers. The page is read-only.

---

### 2.7 Managing Departments

Departments group subjects and teachers under a head of department (Senior Teacher).

1. In the sidebar, click **Departments** (under **CURRICULUM**).
2. Click **+** to create a new department. Enter a **Department Name** and optionally assign a **Head Teacher**.
3. Click **Create**.

Edit and delete controls follow the standard pattern.

**Assigning teachers to a department.** Naming a Head Teacher does not place that teacher in the department. Each teacher is placed in a department through their own account: choose **Department** when you create the teacher (Section 2.2) or edit them later (Section 2.3). Once your institution has at least one department, every Regular and Senior Teacher must have one. A teacher who sees a "You have no department…" banner on their dashboard should ask you to assign one.

---

### 2.8 Managing Houses

Houses are pastoral groups that students can be assigned to. House membership appears on report cards.

1. In the sidebar, click **Houses** (under **CURRICULUM**).
2. Click **+** to create a new house. Enter a **House Name** and optional additional details.
3. Click **Create**.
4. The list shows each house's **Class** column (the classes its students are in, for example _1A, 1B +2_; hover for the full list) and its **Students** count. Deactivated students are not counted.
5. Click on a house row (or its detail link) to view the students assigned to that house. They are listed A–Z by name, with each student's class shown by name.

---

### 2.9 Managing Classes

Classes are the teaching groups that students are enrolled in and teachers are assigned to.

1. In the sidebar, click **Classes** (under **CURRICULUM**).
2. Click **+** to create a new class. Enter the **Class Name** and assign a **Teacher** and any **Students**.
3. Click **Create**.

Edit and delete controls follow the standard pattern.

The class list shows each class's **Population**: the number of active students in it out of its capacity (for example _12 of 30_, in red if the class is over capacity). Deactivated students are not counted. The number is read when the page opens and after a student is saved, moved, imported or deleted; it is not live. The class page (eye button) and a house's page list active students only, so they match.

To see a class at a glance, click the round **eye** button on its row. The class page shows its grade, supervisor and how many students are enrolled (for example _12 of 30_, in red if the class is over capacity), the enrolled students A–Z (click a name to open that student), and the subjects the class studies. Teachers can open this page too; it is read-only.

---

### 2.10 Managing Terms

Terms define the academic periods used for attendance, results, and report cards.

1. In the sidebar, click **Terms** (under **TIMETABLE**).
2. Click **+** to add a new term. Enter the **Term Name**, **Start Date**, and **End Date**.
3. Click **Create**.

> Terms are also set up automatically if you use the Academic Calendar wizard (see Section 2.11). Do not create duplicate terms for the same period.

Click the round **eye** button on a term's row to see its status, start and end dates, length, term number and academic year. The page is read-only.

---

### 2.11 Academic Calendar

The Academic Calendar sets up your school's academic year structure, including the start and end dates of each term and any non-school days (public holidays and closures). Attendance calculations depend on this calendar.

1. In the sidebar, click **Academic Calendar** (under **ADMINISTRATION**).
2. If no academic year has been created yet, you will see a wizard to create one:
   - Enter the **Academic Year Start Date** and **End Date**.
   - The system suggests term start and end dates automatically, adjusting for weekends and any Jamaican public holidays detected. You can override any date manually.
   - Name each term (e.g., "Term 1", "First Term") or accept the suggested names.
   - Click **Create Academic Year** to save.
3. Once an academic year exists, the calendar view shows:
   - The three terms for the selected year.
   - All non-school days (public holidays and manually added closures) highlighted.
4. To add a custom non-school day (e.g., a school closure or internal event), click the relevant date on the calendar and provide a description.

> Accurate academic calendar data is essential for the General Attendance Register and Attendance Summary Register. Set it up before recording attendance.

---

### 2.12 Schedule

The Schedule page shows a weekly timetable view.

1. In the sidebar, click **Schedule** (under **TIMETABLE**).
2. Navigate between weeks using the arrows at the top.
3. Lesson blocks are displayed on their scheduled day and time.

---

### 2.13 Lessons

> **Not available yet.** Lessons cannot be created or edited in the app: the **+** and edit buttons open a short "not available yet" message, and the Lessons list stays empty. Your timetable is built on the **Schedule** page (2.12), and what is taught is recorded in **Lesson Notes** (below).

---

#### Lesson Notes

Lesson Notes are the teaching material (a file and/or links) that teachers share with a class. In the sidebar, click **Lessons**; the page is titled **Lesson Notes**. As an Institution Administrator you see every note in the institution and can create, edit or delete any of them.

1. Click **+**, enter a name, and optionally tick the subjects (up to 5) and the classes (up to 20).
2. Optionally add **up to 12 files** (5 MB each, 15 MB in total) and up to 10 links. A bar shows how much of the 15 MB you have used. If you choose more than fit, the form adds what it can and names the files it left out.
3. Click **Create**. A note is visible to the students and parents of every class you ticked who take at least one of the subjects you ticked (a note with no subject is visible to the whole class); a note with no class is visible to staff only. The table and the note's details list all of its classes. Editing a note lets you add or remove classes.

Only the 100 most recent notes are listed; a message appears when older ones are hidden.

**Downloading all of a note's files.** On a computer, a note with two or more files has **Download all**, which saves one zip. Keep the window open until it finishes; **Stop** asks whether to save what was fetched so far. Afterwards the button becomes **Download remaining**, or **All files downloaded** with **Save again from this device**, which uses no internet data for the files. Until you sign out, a note cannot be fetched in full twice. Phones and tablets use each file's own **Download** button for now. Download all can be switched off for the rest of a month when the system's monthly download allowance is running out; each file's own button keeps working until a later limit.

---

### 2.14 Exams

Exams are scheduled assessments visible to students, parents, and all teacher roles.

1. In the sidebar, click **Exams** (under **TIMETABLE**).
2. Click **+** to add a new exam. Enter the term, subject, class, date, and any additional details. You can also give the exam a **title**, a **type** (Quiz, Test, Exam, End of term Exam, End of year Exam, Mock Exam, Open Book or Other) and a **category** (Multiple Choice is the only one that can be chosen for now; the others are shown as "coming soon"). For Multiple Choice, choose the **answer mode**: Single-answer, Multiple-answer or Both.
3. Click **Create**.

The list shows each exam's title, class, type, teacher, date and, for online exams, its status. Staff see Draft, Scheduled, Open, Closed or Finalized; students and parents see Scheduled, Open or Closed.

**Online exams.** Tick **Online exam** to prepare an exam that students will sit in the app. Set when it opens and closes, the duration (5 to 300 minutes), how scores are released, how often answers are saved, and whether to use Auto-Grade. The form estimates how much of the Exams feature's 10,000 daily writes one sitting of the class will use ("…uses about 24 writes, 0.2% of the 10,000 daily limit, plus about 85 reads"). The form is laid out in sections (**Exam details**, **Type** and **Online exam**); press **Cancel** to close it without saving. This saves a draft.

**Daily limit when publishing.** When you press **Publish** (or **Reopen**), the app adds up the estimated reads and writes of the online exams already set for the same date, with this one, and compares them with 10,000 reads and 10,000 writes a day. It is an estimate, not a count of what the school has really used. If the day is over, a teacher is told to move one of the exams to another day or ask an administrator; an administrator can choose **Yes, publish anyway**. A class whose students have not been counted yet is listed and not included. Once an online exam has questions its category and answer mode can no longer be changed, and a finalized exam cannot be edited.

**Supervision.** The online exam section has a **Supervision** choice. **Supervised (in a room)** is the default: the exam records when a student leaves the page and what they copy, print or open a menu on, switches off copying, the right-click menu, dragging and printing on the questions, and shows you the Integrity column afterwards. **Unsupervised (at home)** does all of that and also asks the browser for fullscreen when the student presses Start (leaving fullscreen is recorded and a button lets them go back), lays a faint mark with the student's name and a short code of their account id over the questions and the zoomed picture (so a photograph of the screen can be traced), and uses a stricter _Review_ mark. For an unsupervised exam you can also tick **Forward only**: students answer in order, each question must be answered before they can move to the next, Previous and the numbered buttons are off, and they cannot come back to a question once they move on (after a reload they reopen on the question after the last one they answered). Nothing blocks a student and the software never penalises one: these are evidence for you to look at. They stop a casual copy only; a screenshot, a photograph, developer tools or another device bypass them. In _Preview as student_ nothing is switched off and you can move freely. A change to Supervision applies to attempts started afterwards. **Writing the questions.** For an online exam the list has three more buttons: **Build questions**, **Preview as student** and **Duplicate exam**. You see them for your own exams, a senior teacher also for the department's, and an administrator for all of them.

- In the builder, press **Add question**, type the question, fill in two to six options and tick the correct one (or ones). Give each question its points (0.5 to 100). In a **Both** exam you choose for each question whether it has one correct answer or several. You can move, duplicate and delete questions; **Delete** asks you to confirm in a window first. Nothing is saved until you press **Save**; saving a draft is always allowed. If you press **Back to exams** with unsaved changes, a window asks whether to leave without saving.
- **Import from file** reads a spreadsheet: press **Download template** (a menu: Excel, CSV, Word, PDF or Markdown), fill in one row per question (`Question`, `Type`, `Option A` to `Option F`, `Correct` as letters such as `B` or `A,C`, `Points`), and choose the file. You see how many questions were read and what to check; if the exam already has questions you choose to replace them or add to the end. The questions open in the builder for you to review and Save. The file is not kept.
- **Word, PDF and Markdown files** can be imported too. Open **Import from file** and read **Word, PDF and Markdown files: the layout to use**: each question starts with its number (`1.` or `1)`; Word's own automatic numbering works), each option is on its own line starting `A.`, `A)` or `(A)`, the right answer is on a line such as `Answer: B` or `Answers: A, C`, and points can be added as `[2 marks]` or `(1 point)`. A long question or option can carry on to the next line, and a title or instructions before question 1 are ignored. **Pictures:** a Word or PDF file can carry them. Put each picture under its question's text and before the first option (in a paragraph of its own or in the question's paragraph); a question keeps one. PNG, JPEG and WebP pictures are read, up to 10 MB each; a GIF, SVG or EMF/WMF picture is not (save it as PNG or JPEG in Word), and pictures in tables and text boxes are ignored. When the file has pictures, **Review the pictures** opens a step that shows each one with the question it was matched to and a _Clear_ or _Check_ mark (_No question_ for one above question 1): use it, move it to another question or remove it, or press **Use all clear ones**. The clear and check pictures must each be decided before you can continue, a question cannot keep two pictures, and a picture on no question is left out unless you move it to one. Word's description of a picture becomes its alt text, which you can change in the builder. In a PDF the pictures painted on the pages are read where they stand; a picture repeated on every page (a logo) or a very small one is left out, a figure drawn with shapes is not a picture and is not found (after choosing the PDF, press **Add a snapshot of a figure**: the page is drawn, you drag a box around the figure or type the box as percentages, choose its question and add it, and it is checked and reviewed like any other picture), and a one-colour stencil image is ignored; PDF files can be up to 25 MB too. In a Markdown file a picture is an image whose address is embedded in the file, written `![description](data:image/png;base64,...)` on its own line under the question or at the end of the question's line; it is placed, reviewed and checked like a Word picture, and the description becomes its alt text. An image with a web address is ignored (a paper should not depend on another server), and so is an image in a table. **Google Forms:** a PDF printed from a Google Form is recognised (its "Mark only one oval." or "Check all that apply." lines and the points at the end of each question); the header and footer of every page, the title, instructions and "Email" item are left out, the options are lettered in order, "Check all that apply." makes a question with several correct answers, and a picture between a question and its options is placed on it. The printout has no answers, so after the pictures step a step called **Choose the right answers** shows each question with its options to tick (you may leave some for the builder, but the exam cannot be published while a question has none). Check the questions in the builder afterwards, in particular options that wrapped onto two lines in the PDF (each line is read as one option). The **Download template** menu gives you a small example in this layout as a Word, PDF or Markdown file to edit. A Markdown (`.md`) file uses the same layout, and headings, **bold**, bullets before the option letters and `\` escapes are tolerated; tables are ignored. The screen then tells you what it found and what to check, such as a table, picture or text box it ignored, or a question with no answer. A PDF must be made from a program such as Word (a scan or photo has no text to read and is refused); an old `.doc` file must be saved as `.docx` first; spreadsheets can be up to 2 MB, and Markdown, PDF and Word files up to 25 MB (they can carry pictures). Check the questions in the builder before you save, especially from a PDF with columns, where the reading order can differ.
- **Publish** makes the exam available to the students of the class when its window opens. It needs a valid title, type, window and duration, at least one question, and, if Auto-Grade is on, a correct answer on every question; the builder lists what is missing. **Back to draft** works only while no student has started; **Close early** stops new attempts; **Reopen** works before the window has ended.
- Until the window opens (and while nobody has started) you can change anything. After that you can only correct which options are right; students keep the questions they have seen, and the key revision goes up so that grades already given show _Re-grade needed_.
- **Pictures.** Under the question text, **Add picture** puts one optional picture on a question (a diagram, a figure, a photo). Choose a file, drop one on the box, or paste one while the box is selected. PNG, JPEG and WebP are accepted, up to 10 MB and 24 million pixels; GIF, SVG and HEIC are not (save the picture as a PNG or JPEG first). The picture is cleaned in your browser: the place and time a photo was taken and other private details are removed, and the picture itself is kept exactly whenever it fits. A line under it says what was done, for example _340 KB, kept exactly_. If a picture is too large to keep, it is made smaller (the line then says _reduced_, with the quality and size); cropping or simplifying the original and adding it again keeps it exactly. A picture too large even reduced is refused with a message. **Replace picture** and **Remove picture** change it. **Alt text** says what the picture shows for a student who cannot see it (it is optional); tick **Decorative** when the picture adds nothing a student needs. A question still needs its text, so it reads correctly where the picture cannot load. Nothing is saved until **Save**; while pictures are saved the button reads _Saving images 3 of 9…_. A line under the title says how many pictures the questions use and about how many megabytes each student downloads for them; pictures count toward the daily limit above, so a paper with many large pictures is better spread over several days. Pictures can be added only while the questions can still be changed, as for the text.
- **Preview as student** shows the exam as a student sees it, one question at a time with the numbered buttons, flags and the answered count. Nothing is saved. It shows the **saved** version of the exam, not changes you have not saved yet (the builder asks before you go to it with unsaved changes). On a phone it is laid out the way a student sees it there (see **Taking an online exam**).
- **The builder on a computer and on a phone.** On a computer the builder has a list of the questions down the side, with each question's number, its first words, buttons to move it up or down, and a mark on the ones that have a problem; choose a question to edit it. **Needs attention (N)** in the list shows only the questions that have a problem, and the toolbar says how many "need attention". On a phone, and on a tablet held upright, the builder shows one question at a time. A strip above it says "Question 2 of 5" and has **Previous**, **Next** and a **Questions** button that opens the same list as a sheet from the bottom. Adding a question shows it at once; deleting one shows the question before it. The toolbar keeps **Save** and the status buttons in view, and puts **Import from file**, **Preview as student** and **Results** behind a **More** menu.
- **Leaving with changes that are not saved.** While the builder has changes you have not saved, going to **Preview as student**, a link in the sidebar, the browser's Back button, **Sign out** or **Start Tour** first asks **Leave without saving?**. **Stay** keeps the page and your changes; **Leave without saving** goes and loses them. Closing the tab asks through the browser's own message. Once you press **Save**, nothing asks. Pressing Back on a phone or Safari's swipe-back is a manual check and may not always ask.

**Deleting an exam.** Only an administrator can delete an exam. For an online exam the confirmation first counts what goes with it (the questions, their pictures, the answer key, and every attempt, score and extra-time entry) and says that scores already copied to the students' results stay. **While any student is still sitting the exam, Delete is refused**: wait until their time is up or they submit. If the deletion stops part-way (for example the connection drops) the exam is still in the list, and pressing Delete again finishes it.

**Marking an online exam.** Once an exam is published, the list has a **Results** button (also on the builder page). The results screen shows how many students started, submitted, were graded and were released, the class average, lowest and highest score, and a table of the class in name order with each student's status, score, percentage, tab switches, extra time and who graded.

**The Exams list and the results on a phone.** On a phone the staff Exams list is a card for each exam instead of a table: the title with an _Online_ mark, the subject, the class and type, the teacher, the date and time, the status, and the buttons (Edit, Delete, Preview as student, Build questions, Results), each big enough for a finger. On the results screen a student's row is also a card, with the score, extra time, re-grade and timeline buttons; **Grade now** and **Release** stay in view, and **Export CSV**, **Show question difficulty**, **Refresh** and **Finalize exam** are in a **More** menu (Finalize is greyed out, with the reason, until the exam has closed, and still asks before it does anything). The question difficulty is a card for each question, and the timeline of a student leaving the page is a list of lines. On a computer the tables and the toolbar are as before.

- **Grading.** If the exam has **Auto-Grade** on and a complete answer key, the submitted attempts are graded for you when you open the screen after the exam has closed (a message says so). **Grade now** grades anything still waiting, and re-grades scores made before you corrected the answer key (those show _Re-grade needed_). A score you entered or changed is never touched by Grade now; use **Re-grade** on that student if you want it recalculated. Attempts a student never finished are marked expired and graded from their last save.
- **Entering and changing scores.** **Enter score** works for any student in the class, including one who never started (for example someone who sat the paper on another day). **Change score** needs a reason, which is kept with the score. You can add a comment the student and parent will see with the score.
- **Extra time.** **Extra time** gives a student up to 120 extra minutes. If they are sitting the exam, their finishing time moves later and their screen updates after its next save.
- **Releasing.** The exam's setting decides when students and parents see a score: as soon as it is graded, at a date and time you chose, or when you press **Release**. A score also goes into the **Results** page (and on to report cards) when it is released; this happens when you open this screen or press Release, so report cards can lag until someone opens it.
- **Integrity.** The table has an **Integrity** column for exams that record the page being left. It shows how many times a student left the exam page (another tab, window or app, or only lost focus), the total time away and the longest spell, an amber **Review** mark when those pass the limit for the exam's length (stricter for an unsupervised exam) (the limits are proposals still being tuned with real sittings, so tell the owner if the mark appears too often or too rarely), and a **Timeline** button that lists each entry with the time into the exam, what happened (left the page, window lost focus, copy, cut, print, context menu, left fullscreen) and how long. Read it as a prompt to look, not a finding: a browser cannot tell a new tab from a switched tab, cannot see where the student went, and a phone call, a notification or a lost connection looks the same as a leave; an empty record is not proof that nothing happened, because the record is written by the student's browser. A score marked before the record existed shows only the old count. When you **Finalize**, the attempts are deleted but a summary and up to 50 timed entries stay on each score, and the Finalize dialog says so. The student and a linked parent are not shown the record, but they can read the summary on a released score through their account.
- **Export CSV** downloads the table (with _Tab switches_, _Time away (seconds)_, _Longest time away (seconds)_ and _Review_). **Show question difficulty** shows, for each question, how many students answered it and how many got it exactly right.
- **Finalize exam** is the last step, once every score is graded, released and in the results. It removes the students' answers (the attempts) and keeps the scores, the results and the question figures; after that the exam cannot be re-graded or edited. If some attempts could not be removed, **Finish clean-up** removes the rest.
- A senior teacher sees the exams of their department, a regular teacher their own, and an administrator all of them. A spectator can view and export but not change anything.
- **Duplicate exam** opens the Add Exam form filled in from the exam; choose the class, date and times, press **Create**, and the questions and answer key are copied into the new draft.

> Only admins can delete exams. Teachers can create and edit exams for their assigned classes.

### 2.15 Assignments

Assignments are coursework tasks due by a specific date, visible to students and parents.

1. In the sidebar, click **Assignments** (under **TIMETABLE**).
2. Click **+** to add a new assignment. Enter the subject, class, due date, and description.
3. Click **Create**.

> Only admins can delete assignments. Teachers can create and edit their own.

> **Deleting an assignment** also deletes every student's submission for it, with each attempt and file. The dialog says how many. Grades already given stay in Results, the gradebook and report cards.

**Rubrics.** Click **Rubrics** (under **TIMETABLE**) to see your school's shared rubrics. Any teacher can type a rubric with **+** or edit one's criteria. A rubric is shared, so editing its criteria changes every assignment using it, including any already graded; the Edit form says so when you change them. Click **Take a tour** for a short guide to the page.

- **Finding a rubric.** Search by title or description, or choose a type: Grid, Grid + file or File only.

- **Viewing.** A rubric shows one criterion at a time, on an assignment's page and when you click the eye icon. Use the arrows, or choose a criterion from the list between them. On a phone or tablet you can also swipe left or right, and on a keyboard you can use the left and right arrow keys.
- **Uploading.** Click **Upload rubric** and choose an Excel (.xlsx), CSV, Word (.docx), PDF or Markdown (.md) file of up to 500 KB. The app reads its template's layout and three others: a list of criteria with one rating scale beside them (such as "5. Excellent"), criteria whose levels sit beside mark ranges (such as "8-10 marks"), and a list of criteria with their allotted marks. If the app finds rubrics in it, it lists them: tick the ones to keep, choose each one's title, tick the box to confirm the grids hold no student names, marks or answer keys, then click **Review grid** to check and correct each before saving. **Only the grids are kept, not the file.** If the app finds no rubric, the file itself is saved, and you can add criteria afterwards with Edit. A PDF with real text is read the same way, one table per page; a scanned PDF has no text to read, so it is saved as a file. A criterion can have no levels, in which case it's marked out of its max points.
- **Templates.** Click **Download Template** for a file in the template's layout, one of the four the app can read: one row for each criterion and one column for each level, with its points in brackets, such as Excellent (4). Replace the examples with your own.
- **What can't be uploaded.** Old Word and Excel files, files with macros, and CSV files with cells that a spreadsheet would run as formulas. An Excel file with formulas gets a warning, because they stay in the copy people download.
- **What not to include.** Don't put student names or marks, or answer keys, in a rubric file. Students and parents can download it exactly as it is. Word and PDF files also carry hidden details, such as the author's name. Students can also technically read any rubric file in your school, not only the ones on their own assignments, so never keep an answer key in a rubric.
- **Confirming a file.** You must tick a box saying a file holds none of these before it can be saved. An Excel file with more than one sheet, or a hidden one, also lists its sheets, because everyone who downloads it gets them all. The app can't check this for you, so make a clean copy if a file has marks or names in it.
- **Replacing and removing.** Only the person who uploaded a file, or an administrator, can replace or remove it. A rubric is shared, so this changes every assignment using it, including any already graded. If a new file holds a grid, you review it, and it replaces the rubric's grid; the new file isn't kept, and the rubric's stored file is removed. A file with no grid replaces the stored file as before. A file can't be removed from a rubric with no criteria; replace or delete the rubric instead. If the criteria are edited after an upload, the page says so, because the two may no longer match.
- **Downloading.** Use the download icon to get the original file, or the criteria as PDF, Excel, Word or Markdown.
- **Deleting.** Administrators can delete any rubric. A teacher can delete a rubric they uploaded: one that holds their own file, or one the app read from a file they uploaded. Assignments using it then show it as missing.

### 2.19 Report Cards

The Report Cards page is where you generate, view, and download student report cards as PDFs.

#### Viewing existing report cards

1. In the sidebar, click **Report Cards** (under **REPORTS**).
2. The table lists all generated report cards for your institution, showing student name, term, class, GPA, and date generated.
3. Click the **PDF icon** on a row to open the PDF preview modal. From there you can download the PDF.

#### Generating a single report card

1. On the Report Cards page, click **Generate Report Card**.
2. Select **Single Student**.
3. Choose a **Student** and a **Term** from the dropdowns.
4. Click **Generate**.
5. If a report card already exists for that student and term, it will be regenerated (updated) rather than duplicated.
6. Any warnings (e.g., missing results for a subject, missing attendance data) are shown after generation. Review these and correct the underlying data if needed.

1. On the Report Cards page, click **Generate Report Card**.
2. Select **Batch (Class)**.
3. Choose a **Class** and a **Term**.
4. Click **Generate All**.

#### The PDF layout

Each report card PDF is a single A4 landscape page designed to fold into a four-panel pamphlet:

- **Front Cover:** Institution logo, student name, class, term, and house.
- **Inner Left:** Attendance summary, extracurricular activities, positions of responsibility, and section comments from supervisory staff.
- **Inner Right:** Subject results table (coursework %, exam %, final %, letter grade, conduct, teacher, and comment number), student average, class average, class rank, and GPA.
- **Back Cover:** Institution details, motto, and authorised signature.

> **Grade calculation note:** If only one component (coursework or exam) has results for a subject, the final grade is calculated entirely from the available component — the missing component is not treated as zero.

### 2.20 General Attendance Register

The General Attendance Register is the primary tool for recording daily student attendance by class, in morning (AM) and afternoon (PM) sessions.

1. In the sidebar, click **General Register** (under **ATTENDANCE**).
2. If prompted by the scope modal, select the **Class** and **Term** you want to record attendance for.
3. The register displays the current week by default, with one column per school day (non-school days are skipped automatically based on your Academic Calendar).
4. Use the week navigation arrows to move to a different week.
5. For each student row, click the attendance button for a given day and session to cycle through states:

   | Code | Meaning |
   | ---- | ------- |
   | P    | Present |
   | A    | Absent  |
   | L    | Late    |
   | S    | Sick    |
   | E    | Excused |

6. For an **Excused** absence, a popover appears where you can record the reason.
7. Attendance is saved as a **draft** automatically as you click. Draft data is stored in your browser temporarily.
8. Click **Submit** to commit the draft to the database. The submit button is only available during the current active session window.

> Sessions from past dates can be edited but within a limited time window. Sessions too far in the past are locked.

**Filling a column quickly:** each session column has a **Fill** button. Click it, pick a state (Present, Absent, Late, Sick or another listed state; Excused is not offered because it needs a reason for each student), and confirm. Only students with no mark yet in that column are filled; anyone you have already marked is left alone. The change is still a draft until you submit.

**Unmarked students:** if you submit (or use **Save All**) while some students still have no mark, the confirmation lists those students by name, per session, before you choose to save anyway. They are not counted in any attendance total.

### 2.23 The Audit Log

The audit log is the permanent record of who changed what. Administrators read it; teachers see only their own entries (Section 1.3).

1. In the sidebar, click **Audit Log** (under **ADMINISTRATION**). (The **Audit and security events** card on your profile shows the latest account and security events, with a **View full audit log** link.)
2. Entries are listed newest first, **20 per page**. Use **Previous** and **Next** to move through older entries; the page number is relative ("Page 2") because there is no cheap way to count every entry.
3. Use the **Event category** menu to narrow the list: **Account & security** (accounts created, deactivated or reactivated, password reset emails, cleared sessions, permission changes, registration decisions), **Academic** (profile edits, photos, attendance, results, feedback comments and lesson notes), **Emails sent** (each time you email someone a sign-in link), or **All events**.
4. Click **Refresh** to re-read the page you are on. Pages are remembered for five minutes so paging back is instant; refresh if you have just made a change and do not see it.

Each entry shows what was done, the event type, who did it, who or what it was done to (`→ Jane Doe`, or a class for attendance and gradebook saves), and when.

**What is recorded**

| You or a teacher…                                                                                   | Is recorded as                                                                                                                                      |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Create an account                                                                                   | Account created                                                                                                                                     |
| Edit a student, teacher or parent                                                                   | Profile edited: names the _fields_ changed ("Updated phone, class for Jane Doe"). Nothing is recorded if nothing changed.                           |
| Deactivate or reactivate an account, clear a sign-in session, change a photo, decide a registration | Account deactivated / reactivated, sign-in session cleared, photo changed, registration status changed                                              |
| Send an administrator a password reset email (super admin)                                          | Password reset sent                                                                                                                                 |
| Grant or revoke schedule-generation access                                                          | Permission changed                                                                                                                                  |
| Save an attendance register                                                                         | Attendance saved: class, date and how many students were recorded. A General Attendance **Save All** records one entry listing the registers saved. |
| Save the gradebook, delete a gradebook column, or add or edit a result                              | Results saved                                                                                                                                       |
| Save a feedback comment (or conduct grades and comments from the gradebook)                         | Feedback comment saved                                                                                                                              |
| Create or edit a lesson note                                                                        | Lesson note saved                                                                                                                                   |

**What is not recorded**

- **Old and new values.** Entries name the fields or records changed and a count, never the old or new phone number, a score or a comment, because entries are permanent and cannot be deleted.
- Creating a gradebook column, editing an institution administrator on **Manage Admins**, and signing in or out.
- Entries cannot be edited or removed by anyone. They are written from the browser, so they show what the app recorded rather than being tamper-proof against a compromised account.

> If a save fails with "Failed to save", the record and its audit entry are written together, so neither was saved. Try again.

Regular Teachers have access to the Subject Attendance Register, which tracks attendance per subject session rather than per school day.

2. Select the **Subject** and **Class** you are taking.
3. The register shows each student in the class.
4. Mark each student as **Present**, **Absent**, **Late**, or **Excused** for this session. To mark everyone who has no mark yet on a date, use that date's **Fill** button (Excused is not offered).
5. Click **Submit** to save. If some students are still unmarked, the confirmation names them before you choose to save anyway.

### 3.11 Your Recorded Actions

When you save an attendance register, the gradebook, a result, a feedback comment or a lesson note, the system records that you did (the class, subject and date, and how many students, never the scores or comment text). Administrators can see these in the **Audit Log**.

You can see your own entries on your profile: open **User Profile**, then the **Actions I performed** tab on the **Activity** card. You cannot see other people's entries, and entries cannot be edited or deleted. See Section 2.23 for the full list of what is recorded.

### 4.3 Exams and Assignments

You can view the assignments for your class.

1. Click **Assignments** (under **TIMETABLE**) to see all assignments and their due dates. On a phone each assignment is a card with a coloured due-date label (red when overdue, amber when due within three days).
2. Click **Exams** (under **TIMETABLE**) to see the exams of your classes: the date, the time allowed, a status (_Scheduled_, _Open now_, _In progress_, _Submitted_, _Result released_, _Missed_ or _Closed_) and, once your teacher has released it, your score.

These lists are read-only. Contact your teacher if details appear incorrect.

If an assignment has a rubric, its page shows one criterion at a time. A short note above it explains how to use it. Use the arrows or the list between them, or swipe left or right on a phone or tablet. Click **Download rubric** to keep a copy.

**Taking an online exam.**

1. When an exam shows **Open now**, press **Start**. The start screen shows the number of questions, the time you have (and any extra time your teacher gave you), when the exam closes, and the rules. If you start late you get what is left until the exam closes, and the screen tells you how many minutes that is. On a phone it suggests a larger screen; you can still continue. The start screen also says what the exam records: that it notes when you leave the page (by switching to another tab or window) and when you copy or print, and that your teacher can see it.
2. Press **Start exam** (in an unsupervised exam this also asks your browser for fullscreen; a **Return to fullscreen** button appears if you leave it, and your name is shown faintly over the questions). You can attempt the exam once, and the timer cannot be paused. The countdown runs from the server's clock, not your device's.
3. Answer one question at a time. Use the numbered buttons to jump to any question, **Flag for review** to mark one to come back to (it only marks the question for you: it does not change your answer, and flagged questions are highlighted in the list of numbers), and **Previous** and **Next**. Exam times are shown as "3:00 PM on April 14, 2026" in Jamaica time (a note says so when your device is in another time zone). Hover over the Flag button, or tap the small **i** icon beside it on a phone or tablet, to see what it does. A question that says "Select all that apply" has several correct answers. The screen shows how many questions you have answered. If a question has a picture, it appears under the question; tap or click it to see it larger (**Actual size** shows every pixel and lets you scroll or pinch). Pictures load in the background and never stop you answering: if one does not load, press **Try again** next to it.
4. Your answers are saved to the server every few minutes and when you submit; they are also kept in your browser. If the page closes or you refresh, open the exam again and press **Resume**: you return to the same questions in the same order with the same finishing time. Leaving the tab or window is counted and your teacher can see the number; nothing is blocked.
5. **Submit exam** is off until every question has an answer; beside it the screen says how many are left (a flag is not an answer). In an exam that is **forward only**, **Next** is also off until the question you are on has an answer, so choose one even if you are unsure. Press **Submit exam** when you are done; the confirmation tells you how many questions are flagged. When time is up the screen says **Time's up!**, your answers are fixed and they are submitted for you, even if some questions have no answer; if the connection is down it says so and keeps trying while you stay on the page. After that the page says your result will appear when your teacher releases it; the score, the percentage and any comment appear in the list and on this page once released. You never see the questions or the answers afterwards.

**On a phone, or a tablet held upright.** The exam shows one question at a time. The clock, the position ("Question 3 of 20 · 5 answered") and, when something needs your attention, what is saved stay at the top, and the buttons stay at the bottom, however far you scroll the question. The bottom row is **Previous**, **Questions** (with a number for how many are not answered) and **Next**, which becomes **Submit exam** on the last question. **Questions** opens a list of numbered buttons (answered, not answered, flagged); choose one to go to it. You can also swipe left for the next question and right for the previous one: a swipe that begins inside a dialog (a picture opened larger is one) or is made with two fingers does nothing, and in a **forward only** exam only the swipe to the next question works, and only once the question has an answer. If your device is set to reduce motion the question changes without sliding. When time is up a red **Time's up!** banner says your answers are being sent, the buttons turn off, and the exam submits for you.
